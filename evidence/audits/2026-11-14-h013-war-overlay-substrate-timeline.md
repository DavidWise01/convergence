# H013 — Electrical pulse to ferrite-core memory, war-overlay chronology
Recorded: 2026-11-14
Status: historical context, not proof of causal or physical network continuity.

## Substrate anchors
- **1832:** Pavel Schilling's publicly demonstrated electromagnetic telegraph. This is the selected *electrical encoded-signal* anchor, not the invention of electricity, all communication, or silicon devices.
- **1906:** Silicon crystal rectifiers were in use for radio detection; separate from the 1832 electrical signaling root.
- **1947:** Point-contact transistor amplification demonstrated at Bell Labs.
- **1953:** MIT Whirlwind I introduced coincident-current ferrite-core main memory (first bank installed August 8). A 32×32 plane contains 1,024 magnetic bits, addressed/read/written through copper wiring. Ferrite is the magnetic storage medium; copper is the access wiring; magnetrons are microwave generators, not core-memory elements.

## Interpretation boundary
Military conflict can be a **context for investment, deployment, and adaptation**. Chronological alignment alone does not establish any causal origin of a specific modern algorithm or continuous functioning of a particular wire, processor, memory plane, or computer.
Different war dates, technology substrate dates and adapter generations must be kept on distinct axes. In particular, 1953 was an important *memory transition*, not the conclusion of WWII or the beginning of the Cold War.
